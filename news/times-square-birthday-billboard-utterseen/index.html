A 20-year-old founder put a birthday wish on a Times Square billboard to launch an AI app

November 19, 2026

In short: The Times Square billboard that reads "hey i'm fay im 20, i turn 21 soon, for my birthday please try utterseen.com" is a launch ad for Utterseen, a voice AI assistant for Mac and Windows that can see your screen. Its 20-year-old founder skipped the usual logo, features and slogan and asked for one birthday present instead.

What does the Times Square billboard say?

Four lines of plain lowercase text on white panels: "hey i'm fay im 20 / i turn 21 soon / for my birthday please try / utterseen.com." No product shot, no logo, no feature list. In a square full of motion and celebrity ads, it reads more like a text message than a commercial, which is exactly why people notice it.

Why a birthday instead of a normal ad?

Asking strangers to download an app is awkward. Asking for one thing on your birthday is something everyone has done. The ad turns a product launch into a personal request, and it asks people to try, not buy, which fits: Utterseen has a free plan.

Why are people arguing about it?

Anti-ads split people. Critics say it's begging and tells you nothing about the product. Fans say it's honest, impossible to mistake for anything else, and does the one job a billboard can do: make you curious enough to type a web address. The debate has spilled onto Reddit, which is its own kind of reach.

So what is Utterseen?

Utterseen is a voice AI assistant for Mac and Windows that sees your screen. You hold one key, ask out loud, and hear the answer in whatever app you're using, with no chat window and no copying and pasting. Students use it to make study guides and flashcards from their slides and to get homework explained step by step. Job seekers say "apply to this job," and it makes a resume tailored to that job, fills out the application, and stops before Submit.

Does the stunt work?

A billboard can get someone to type a URL once. Whether they stay depends on the app. The bet behind this one is simple: an AI that answers about whatever is on your screen is easier to show than to describe, so the ad doesn't try to describe it at all.
