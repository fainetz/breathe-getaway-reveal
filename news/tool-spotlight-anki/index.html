Tool spotlight: Anki, the free flashcard app medical students swear by

November 19, 2026

In short: Anki is a free, open-source flashcard app that uses spaced repetition, showing each card right before you're likely to forget it, so you remember more in less review time. It's especially popular with medical and language students. The desktop app and Android app are free; the iPhone app is a one-time purchase.

Why does spaced repetition work?

Reviewing a fact just before you'd forget it makes the memory last longer each time. Anki schedules every card for you, so easy cards come back rarely and hard ones come back often. Newer versions include the FSRS scheduler, which predicts forgetting more precisely.

Why do medical students love it?

Huge amounts of facts, years of retention needed, and shared decks built by other students. Many med school communities trade well-known decks.

What's the catch?

Making cards takes time, and the interface feels dated. The fix for the first one is letting AI make the cards: give ChatGPT your notes and import the result, or use Utterseen, which makes Anki flashcards straight from slides or notes on your screen.

How do you start?

Download Anki, make or import a deck, and review every day, even for ten minutes. Consistency matters more than session length. Our guide: how to make flashcards with AI.

Questions people ask

Is Anki free?

The desktop app and AnkiDroid are free. AnkiMobile for iPhone is a one-time purchase that supports development.

Is Anki better than Quizlet?

Anki has stronger spaced repetition for long-term memory. Quizlet is easier to use and share. Both import AI-made cards.

Can AI make Anki cards?

Yes. ChatGPT can write question and answer pairs to import, and Utterseen can make Anki flashcards from slides or notes on your screen.
