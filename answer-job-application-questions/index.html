How to answer common job application questions fast

November 18, 2026

Short answer: To answer job application questions fast, write a reusable answer for each common question once (why this company, why you're a fit, salary expectations, availability) and adapt one or two sentences per job. Keep answers short and specific; most forms are skimmed.

How do you answer "Why do you want to work here?"

Two sentences: one specific thing about the company (a product, a mission, how they work) and how it connects to what you want to do. Specific beats flattering. Swap the company detail for each application and keep the rest.

How do you answer "Why are you a good fit?"

Pick the job's top two requirements and give one real example for each, with a number if you have one. That's it. The form isn't the interview.

What do you put for salary expectations?

Look up the typical range for the role and city on sites that publish pay data or in the posting itself, since many states now require ranges in job ads. Give a range you'd be happy with, or "open, based on the full role," if the form allows text.

What about the yes or no questions?

Work authorization, sponsorship, start date, willingness to relocate: these are the same on every form. Keep your answers in one document so you don't have to think about them each time.

Filling these in for you

Utterseen is a free AI assistant for Mac and Windows that sees your screen. On an application, hold a key and say "fill this out," and it answers the form from your resume, including the written questions. It never presses Submit unless you tell it to.

Questions people ask

How long should answers on a job application be?

Short. Two to four sentences for written questions is usually enough. Recruiters skim, so lead with the specific point.

Should I leave salary expectations blank?

If the field is optional, you can. If required, give a researched range for the role and location, or a flexible answer if text is allowed.

Can AI answer job application questions?

Yes. It can draft answers from your resume and the job posting. Make sure every claim is true before you submit.
