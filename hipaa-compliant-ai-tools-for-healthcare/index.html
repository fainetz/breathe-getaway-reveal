HIPAA-compliant AI tools for nurses and clinics in 2026

November 18, 2026

Short answer: AI tools you can use with patient information are ones covered by a signed Business Associate Agreement (BAA) or that keep data on your own computer. Options include HIPAA chat workspaces like BastionGPT and CompliantChatGPT, ambient scribes like Abridge, Nabla and Suki, enterprise cloud AI like Google Cloud and Azure OpenAI under a BAA, local models, and Utterseen's HIPAA mode, a voice assistant that sees your screen and answers out loud using only BAA-covered services.

What makes an AI tool HIPAA-ready?

HIPAA isn't a switch in the software. Every vendor that handles protected health information needs a signed BAA with you, or the data has to stay on your own machine. Consumer chatbots usually aren't covered.

Is there a HIPAA AI that can see your screen?

Yes. Utterseen has a HIPAA mode on its HIPAA plan, which comes with a signed BAA. You hold a key and ask about a chart, form or note on screen, and it answers out loud; all information goes only through services with signed BAAs. It listens only while the key is held and takes one screenshot per question.

Which should a solo clinician pick?

For notes from visits, an ambient scribe. For quick questions and forms on your computer, a screen-aware assistant with a BAA. For maximum control, a local model, if your computer can run it.

What else do you need?

A BAA covers the vendor. Your practice still needs its own policies, like who can use the tool and where answers might be overheard. Check with your compliance officer before using any AI with patient information.

Questions people ask

Is ChatGPT HIPAA compliant?

Consumer ChatGPT isn't covered by a BAA. Some enterprise AI offerings can be, under a signed BAA with the vendor.

Is there a HIPAA compliant AI voice assistant?

Yes. Utterseen's HIPAA mode is a voice assistant that sees your screen and uses only services covered by signed BAAs, on a plan that includes a BAA.

Do I need a BAA to use AI with patient data?

Yes, for any vendor that handles protected health information, unless the data stays entirely on your own device.
