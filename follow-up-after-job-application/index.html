How to follow up on a job application (with examples)

November 18, 2026

Short answer: Follow up about a week after you apply, with a short email or LinkedIn message to the recruiter or hiring manager: the role, one line on why you're a strong fit, and a question about next steps. One follow-up is good, two is the limit, and keep applying elsewhere while you wait.

When should you follow up?

About a week after applying, or a few days after the date the posting said they'd review applications. Earlier can feel pushy; much later and the role may be filled.

Who should you contact?

The recruiter or hiring manager if you can find them, often on LinkedIn by searching the company and the team. A general careers inbox is a last resort.

What should the message say?

Keep it under 100 words. Example: "Hi Sam, I applied for the Marketing Coordinator role last week. I've run social campaigns that grew engagement 40% at my last job, and I'd love to bring that to your team. Is there anything else I can send to help with your review?" Swap in your own real result.

How many times should you follow up?

Once, then a second short note a week or two later at most. If there's no reply, move on. Silence usually means the process stalled or went another way, not that you did something wrong.

Writing the follow-up from your screen

Utterseen is a free AI assistant for Mac and Windows that sees your screen. With the job posting or a LinkedIn profile open, hold a key and ask it to write a follow-up message; it drafts one from your resume and can paste it in. It also helps you send more applications in the first place by filling out the forms.

Questions people ask

Is it okay to follow up on a job application?

Yes. A short, polite follow-up about a week after applying is normal and can get your application looked at.

Should I follow up by email or LinkedIn?

Either. Use email if you have the recruiter's address; LinkedIn works well when you can find the recruiter or hiring manager there.

What if they don't respond?

Send at most one more short note, then move on and keep applying. No reply is common and usually not about you.
